# Publishing an Edition

Publication is two switches, and both must be on.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The text** must be public | [Manage](/editor/opening) → Texts → the **Public** switch beside the title |
| **The edition** must be public | The **Public** toggle on the edition's row in the Editions tab |

Until both are set, the edition exists only in the Editor. The **Share** menu in the header tells you
which one is missing — its items are disabled, and hovering explains: *This text is private. Make the
text public to open it in the Viewer.*

## Why two

They control different things. A public text makes the manuscript and its images available; a public
edition makes one particular reading available. That lets you publish a finished edition of a
manuscript while a second, in-progress edition of the same fragment stays private.

The Editor fails closed: anything not explicitly public is treated as private, so an edition is never
exposed by an ambiguous setting.

## Before you publish

Publication makes the edition readable by anyone with the link, so it is worth a pass through the
[report](/viewer/edition-report) first — it shows the whole edition in one scroll, in all three
display modes, and unfinished work is obvious there in a way it is not in the Editor.

Worth checking:

- **Tokens with no lemma.** Their cartouches have no second line in the transcription pane.
- **The edition's Name**, which is what readers see in the Viewer's edition dropdown.
- **Translation and Chāyā** on the edition, on sequences, or both — whichever the edition uses.
- **Text-critical marks**, since they drive what the diplomatic and reconstructed readings show.

## Unpublishing

Turning either switch off withdraws the edition. Existing links stop resolving. Nothing is deleted,
and turning it back on restores access.

## Deleting

Deleting an edition removes it and everything in it; deleting a text removes every edition and image
belonging to it. Neither can be undone, and both are offered only from the **⋮** menus, behind a
confirmation naming what is about to go.
