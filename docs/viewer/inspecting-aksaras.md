# Inspecting an Aksara

With the header set to **Syllable** scope, selecting a single aksara — on the image or in the text —
opens it in the Details pane.

## What Details shows

- **The reading**, at the top, as the edition transliterates it.
- **A cut-out** of that aksara, clipped from the manuscript image at its recorded outline. This is
  the actual writing, not a font.
- **Segment metadata**, if the editor recorded any for this aksara.

Aksaras with nothing surviving on the surface — restorations across a lacuna — have no cut-out to
show, so none appears.

## Enlarging it

Click the cut-out, or the magnifier beside it, to open the aksara on its own.

![The aksara inspector showing an enlarged cut-out of the sign ya](/images/viewer/aksara-inspect.jpg)

Inside the dialog:

- **Zoom in / Zoom out** step the magnification.
- **Reset** returns to the fitted view.
- **Drag** to move around when zoomed in.

Close it with the ✕, the <kbd>Esc</kbd> key, or a click outside. This is the tool for judging a
doubtful sign: it goes well past the resolution at which the aksara is legible in the image pane.

## When to use it

Syllable scope answers questions about the writing rather than the language — is this really *ṣa*
rather than *sa*, is that stroke a vowel diacritic or damage, does this sign match the hand
elsewhere. For the third question, [the paleography view](/viewer/paleography-view) puts every
instance of a syllable side by side, and is usually faster than inspecting them one at a time.

For lexical and grammatical information, switch the header to **Token** scope — see
[Word and Lemma Details](/viewer/word-details).
